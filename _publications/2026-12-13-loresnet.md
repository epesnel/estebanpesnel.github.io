<div class="sp-section">
<div class="sp-section-title">Abstract</div>
<div class="sp-abstract">
Neural pre-processing can substantially improve the rate-distortion efficiency of Adaptive Bitrate (ABR) video streaming, but existing solutions either require non-standard client-side filters or are too complex for real-time deployment on CPU. In this context, we propose <strong>LoResNet</strong>, a lightweight learned downscaler architecture paired with a standard, fixed bicubic upsampler. Codec-awareness is learned once by a high-capacity teacher trained through the codec via a state-of-the-art surrogate gradient. We find that this knowledge transfers to lightweight architectures by <strong>distilling in the latent (pre-codec) domain</strong>, removing the codec from student training and enabling fast, exact-gradient learning that preserves the teacher's gains. This makes a broad architecture sweep affordable, yielding a (FLOPs, BD-Rate) Pareto frontier and three deployment-ready points: LoResNet-fast, -medium, and -slow, spanning an order of magnitude in complexity. The fast point requires only <strong>1.48 GFLOPs</strong>, up to <strong>403&times; fewer operations</strong> than the ProgDown-Lite teacher, and processes 1080p at <strong>165 fps on a CPU</strong> (up to &sim;10&times; faster than the teacher), while still delivering consistent BD-Rate gains over Lanczos across x264, x265 and VVenC, up to <strong>-8.77% in PSNR</strong> and <strong>-18.45% in VMAF</strong>.
</div>
</div>

<!-- ════════════ METHOD ════════════ -->
<div class="sp-section">
<div class="sp-section-title">Key Idea: Distilling Codec-Awareness in the Latent Domain</div>
<p>Training a codec-aware downscaler end-to-end (e.g. with a surrogate gradient such as <a href="/publication/scaled">SCALED</a>) requires running the actual codec in the training loop, which is affordable for a single high-capacity teacher but prohibitive for sweeping many lightweight student architectures. LoResNet decouples the two: a teacher is trained once, through the codec, to produce codec-aware low-resolution outputs. Lightweight students are then trained to match the teacher's <strong>pre-codec latent representation</strong> directly, via a standard exact-gradient regression loss - no codec, no surrogate, no per-architecture codec calls. This turns an expensive per-model codec-in-the-loop search into a cheap architecture sweep, while preserving most of the teacher's rate-distortion gains.</p>
</div>
